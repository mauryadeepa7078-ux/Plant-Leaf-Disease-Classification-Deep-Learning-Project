Project notes

A careful classroom model, not a field oracle.

LeafGuard AI is an academic computer-vision project that classifies 16 PlantVillage leaf classes across four crops. It is designed to show a complete, reproducible deep-learning workflow — including its limits.

Objective

What this project sets out to do

Many plant-disease demos stop at a single crop or hide an untrained frontend behind random labels. LeafGuard AI instead asks a narrower, testable question: can a transfer-learning model separate 16 real PlantVillage classes across tomato, apple, grape and corn, and report its performance honestly?

The project covers the full path from dataset verification, cleaning and leakage-aware splitting through training, held-out evaluation, a real inference pipeline and a Flask interface. The website never invents a prediction: every uploaded image is passed through the saved model.

Problem statement

Why multi-plant classification is harder

A tomato-only classifier can learn one leaf shape, one background style and four labels. A 16-class, four-crop model must discriminate diseases that look similar across species while also learning what a healthy leaf looks like for each crop. PlantVillage’s controlled images make that feasible, but they do not represent every field condition.

Approach

From verified folders to a measured model

1

Verify all 16 folders

Use the exact PlantVillage folder names. Stop with an explicit error if any class is missing rather than substituting or inventing a label.

2

Clean, deduplicate and split

Reject corrupt files, remove exact duplicates, group perceptual near-duplicates, resize to 224 × 224 RGB and use StratifiedGroupKFold for 70/15/15 splits.

3

Transfer learning

Freeze MobileNetV2, extract features with training-only augmentation, then train a regularised classifier head with early stopping, learning-rate reduction and checkpointing.

4

Evaluate and connect

Score the untouched test split, save confusion matrix and per-class metrics, then load the same saved model in Flask with the same preprocessing.

Reliability

What the confidence score means

Confidence is the softmax score for the highest class. It is not the probability that the plant is truly diseased. A score below 60%, or a top-two margin below 15%, marks the result as uncertain. The app also checks resolution, brightness, contrast and focus before trusting an upload.

Even a high-confidence answer can be wrong when the photo differs from the controlled PlantVillage images. That is why every result page includes a limitations reminder and why treatment decisions should be confirmed by an expert.

Limitations & disclaimer

What this model cannot do

These limitations are part of the result, not an afterthought.

01

Dataset conditions

PlantVillage images were collected under controlled lighting with plain backgrounds. Scores on those images can be much higher than on real farm photos.

02

Image dependence

Background, lighting, blur, leaf age and camera quality can all change a prediction. The app filters some unusable images, but cannot detect every out-of-distribution photo.

03

Similar-looking classes

Some diseases have overlapping symptoms, and the corn Cercospora/gray-leaf-spot classes are combined in PlantVillage. The confusion matrix exposes these confusions.

04

No field validation

The model has not been validated across regions, cultivars, seasons or smartphone cameras. Real deployment would require a field-collected, expert-labelled dataset.

05

Not a diagnosis

A confidence score is not a guarantee of diagnostic accuracy. Do not use LeafGuard AI alone to decide whether to apply pesticide or destroy a crop.

06

Educational scope

The project demonstrates a deep-learning workflow for learning and assessment. It is not a commercial decision-support system.

Use responsibly. Always confirm a suspected disease with a qualified agricultural professional and follow local extension guidance. Pesticide use should comply with the product label and local regulations.
